# Three outline choices

Prepared 18 October 2026 using blog-think and the owner's supplied notes. The owner explicitly requested three alternatives; this takes precedence over the skill's usual one-to-two-option default. These are alternative directions for development, not three approved articles. No full article or experiment has been created.

## Shared thought brief

Reader question: What does responsible reading mean when humans and agents can work through summaries, reusable skills and long conversations?

Provisional view: Both people and agents need the current context behind an action. AI can help process material, while the decision-maker remains responsible for understanding the relevant evidence, constraints and next step. Packaging procedures into skills does not certify that current task evidence was consulted.

Reasons to explore: easier production can shift work to recipients; navigation supplies a useful analogy for delegated judgment; Iqra gives the owner a personal reflective anchor; agent workflows make the distinction between stored knowledge and active task context concrete. These are separate strands, not a demonstrated causal chain.

Strongest alternative: delegation can improve access, explanation and focus. A reader may understand a decision through selective source checks and AI-assisted explanation without manually reading every word. An agent may already hold a verified current passage, making another retrieval unnecessary.

What could change the view: direct evidence of better understanding under AI assistance should narrow a claim about loss; an observed agent trace may show a tool failure or an ambiguous requirement rather than a context problem. None of these outlines depends on proving that nobody reads anymore or that every long session deteriorates.

## 1. Read Before You Decide

Genre and shape: personal reflection. Move from an open question through the navigation analogy and the author's interpretation to a modest personal commitment. This uses the project's reflective arc; it does not claim a full application of Gibbs's work, which was only inspected through a publisher record.

Reader question: What should I still engage with when AI can provide the summary?

Central idea: reading is deliberate engagement with the context that makes a decision meaningful. The connection to Iqra is the owner's contemporary reflection, with appropriate religious context.

1. **An answer can arrive before understanding.** Open with the question of what remains to be read after receiving a useful AI summary. Add an actual author-supplied incident only if one is later provided.
2. **Arriving and knowing the route.** Use the supplied Google Maps/Waze analogy to explore destination, alternatives and attention to surroundings. Treat it as an analogy; do not assert proprietary routing behavior or invent a trip.
3. **Iqra as an invitation to engage.** Introduce the owner's reflection on reading before deciding, grounded in the verse and first-revelation account. Acknowledge earlier Iqra–AI writing when developing this section.
4. **Where delegation helps.** Consider summaries and explanations that let people understand material they might otherwise struggle to approach. Distinguish responsible selection from a requirement to read every line.
5. **The same question reaches the agent.** Briefly connect the human habit to checking whether an AI consulted the current brief. Mention skills and summaries as aids without turning the reflection into a technical tutorial.
6. **A small commitment before acting.** End with checking the decisive passage, explaining the reasoning in one's own words, and naming what remains uncertain. Leave room for the unresolved tension between convenience and attention.

Development needed: an optional real moment in the owner's words; the owner's preferred balance between the religious reflection and the work discussion. Source plan: S3–S4 for textual context; S2 only if making a navigation-research claim; S6 for a bounded positive AI result; S7–S9 for the short agent connection. Compare V3–V6 to credit existing framing. Avoid copying their personal stories.

What would change the piece: a useful account of AI increasing engagement should become part of the reflection rather than an exception concealed at the end. No empirical conclusion about population-wide reading decline is required.

## 2. When Everyone Can Write, Who Still Understands?

Genre and shape: argument-led essay. Answer the reader's question through grouped reasons and then test the strongest challenge, adapting the organizing approach credited to [Barbara Minto](https://www.barbaraminto.com/). The structure helps explain an argument; it does not validate the claims.

Reader question: How can a team tell whether AI-assisted work has produced enough shared understanding to act?

Central idea: the value of a work document depends partly on whether its recipient can interpret the decision, constraints and next action. Higher output alone is an incomplete measure of progress.

1. **The document is finished; the handoff may still be unclear.** Establish the gap as a question or clearly illustrative scenario. Use a real anonymized example only if supplied.
2. **The recipient's work.** Explore checking substance, reconstructing context and asking follow-up questions. Use V1's workslop discussion within its survey limits; useful documents can also compete for finite attention, which remains an evidence gap here.
3. **A summary needs enough context to support action.** Examine missing assumptions, changing requirements and unclear decisions. Distinguish the owner's concerns from measured effects on memory or focus.
4. **A chain involving both people and agents.** Follow the producer, assistant, document and recipient. Introduce current sources and reusable skills as possible support for a clear handoff; locate responsibility for the action.
5. **The strongest challenge: AI may reduce the burden.** Weigh concise explanations and better-quality writing against unnecessary volume. Consider management habits and pre-existing overload as competing explanations.
6. **Define a useful handoff.** Propose naming the decision, decisive source/version, relevant constraints, owner and next action. End with an observable standard for understanding rather than a document count.

Development needed: an actual handoff example would sharpen the argument. Direct evidence is still needed for the proposed chain from output volume to skimming, weaker recall and poorer decisions. Source plan: V1–V2 for existing workplace positions; S1 and S6 with their inspection limits; S7–S9 for agent context. Complete deeper appraisal before using the partially inspected studies to carry strong factual claims.

What would change the piece: evidence that AI reduces recipient effort or improves comprehension should change the conclusion about which work practices help. The essay should not presume that more AI use always creates worse coordination.

## 3. The Agent Has a Skill. Did It Read the Source?

Genre and shape: explanatory essay with a proposed practice. Move between the current situation and a better source-checking workflow, adapting the change-oriented approach credited to [Nancy Duarte](https://www.duarte.com/blog/why-resonate/). It becomes a field note only if a real test is later performed and documented.

Reader question: How can an agent have the right procedure and still act on the wrong task context?

Central idea: reusable know-how, current project facts and the material available in this response are different things. A skill should help an agent locate and use the relevant evidence.

1. **A capable answer to the wrong version of the task.** Introduce a clearly hypothetical changed requirement. Do not imply that an incident or benchmark has already occurred.
2. **Three places information can live.** Explain model training, external documents or persistent memory, and active context. Focus on their practical relationship rather than architecture detail.
3. **What a skill contributes.** Show how procedures, references and tools can be packaged and loaded when needed. Separate reusable guidance from facts that change with the current brief.
4. **What “lazy” might look like.** Discuss skipped checks, repeated assumptions and missed constraints. Distinguish accumulated irrelevant material, compaction loss and other possible failures; avoid diagnosing a platform from a long conversation alone.
5. **Return to the current source.** Propose a workflow: identify the objective, choose the skill, inspect the relevant document/version, reconcile it with current instructions, and check the output against the decisive passage. Preserve a verified unchanged copy when appropriate.
6. **Make the claim observable.** Describe a possible later comparison using a revised document before and after a long interaction. Explain what evidence would distinguish a missed source from a mistaken interpretation. Close with the owner's Iqra reflection as a reason to keep consulting evidence.

Development needed: decide whether to include an illustrative workflow only or later perform a small documented demonstration. No benchmark or causal test has been run. Source plan: S7–S10 for engineering guidance, skills format and bounded context findings; S11 only as an explicitly limited author summary. TC01–TC09 retain the technical and interpretive boundaries.

What would change the piece: a trace showing current evidence was already available and used would weaken the case for another document read. A failed tool call or conflicting user requirements would require a different explanation. A single demonstration cannot establish that compaction or context length generally causes failures.

## Recommendation and next step

Choose **1** for the first personal-journal entry: it most directly carries the owner's navigation analogy, Iqra reflection and concern about reading, while keeping the technical section proportionate. Choose **2** for the workplace argument, or **3** if the main goal is to explain agent behavior and a practical method.

Keep the other directions as possible later pieces, not an automatic series or a publishing commitment. After the owner selects an outline, develop that direction and resolve its defining evidence gaps before final editorial approval.
