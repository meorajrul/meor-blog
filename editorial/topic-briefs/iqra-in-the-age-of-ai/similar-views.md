Prepared 18 October 2026. Topic research only; no article has been drafted or published. This is a curated comparison of inspected public writing, not an exhaustive review or a measure of how common these views are. Author positions below are paraphrases. Comparisons and recommendations are editorial interpretations.

## What the search changed

Both the GPS metaphor and the explicit Iqra–AI connection have precedents. Hasrizal used the latter framing in August 2025; Hafiz Hanif has an especially close title. Neither connection should be presented as an original discovery. The useful opportunity is a specific personal reflection on workplace understanding, decision responsibility, and the requirement for an AI assistant to consult task evidence.

Meor's supplied position combines four concerns: easier document production can outrun attention; summaries can obscure the context needed to act; navigation offers an analogy for delegated thinking; and Iqra inspires deliberate engagement with knowledge. Meor also applies the context requirement to the AI harness itself. No measured reading decline or personal incident has been supplied.

## How to develop Meor's argument

A defensible provisional thesis is: AI can help us process documents, but a decision still requires engagement with its sources, constraints and uncertainties. Humans need enough understanding to own the decision; AI assistants need relevant evidence for the particular task. These are related responsibilities, not equivalent cognitive processes.

The contribution would be this application and the author's own reflection. The search does not establish that the combination is unique. Credit the earlier writers if drawing on their framing. The current working title is very close to V6; consider a more specific title when drafting, such as **When Everyone Can Write, Who Still Understands?**

A possible practical ending would ask whether the decision-maker can explain the issue, identify the source/version, state the important constraints, and name the next action. An AI assistant could help by retrieving passages, exposing contradictions and flagging missing evidence. This is a proposed practice, not an evaluated intervention.

Avoid asserting that nobody reads, that output grows exponentially, or that AI necessarily damages memory. These are stronger claims than this comparison establishes. A real anonymized episode would give the reflection substance without inventing a general trend.

## Evidence boundaries and stopping decision

V2–V6 and V8 are essays or personal/practitioner views, not proof of a population-wide effect. V1 reports an online workplace survey on a commercial research page; its findings do not establish the entire chain from document volume to skimming, recall and decision quality. V7 is vendor engineering guidance, not a study of human reading. Religious and historical interpretations in V5–V6 are attributed to those authors; their underlying references were not independently audited in this pass.

The positions now cover close agreement, faith-based parallels, a matching metaphor with the opposite emphasis, and the technical context point. That is sufficient for the requested comparison. A causal claim about workplace reading would require further direct evidence. Access details, dates and locators are in similar-views-sources.json.
