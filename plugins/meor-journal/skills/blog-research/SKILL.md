# Blog Research


Read [the packet contract](../../references/editorial-contract.md). Begin with the article brief and evidence gaps. Keep the thesis provisional. For personal reflection, research only external factual claims; the web cannot supply the author's memories.

Use the requested autoresearch rhythm in a bounded form. Inner loop: choose one consequential gap, state what would support/challenge it, search, inspect the source, appraise it, map the claim, and record what changed. Outer loop: after meaningful checks or a surprise, synthesize support, conflicts, uncertainty and the next question; deepen, broaden, pivot or conclude.

Search original studies, authoritative records, official technical documentation or relevant primary evidence. Record actual queries, dates, filters and inclusion/exclusion reasons in the search log. Investigate source ownership and underlying evidence. Many articles repeating one study are one origin group. An abstract-only or inaccessible item must retain that status and cannot support a claim requiring full-text inspection.

For each cited source, inspect the actual relevant passage and record URL, date, precise locator, publisher/author, origin group and appraisal. Check method, sample, measurement, comparator, uncertainty, conflicts and applicability. Search for contradictory evidence or alternative explanations and document the result even when none is found. Do not infer causation from association or generalize a personal observation to everyone.

Fill sources.json and claims.json; retain data and transformations for calculations. Cite the inspected source beside its supported statement in the article. Narrow or remove unsupported claims. Distinguish external fact, inference, calculation, opinion and personal experience.

Conclude when all material claims have appropriate inspected evidence, counterevidence has been sought, the conclusion fits the scope, and two targeted follow-up passes add no consequential change. This stop rule is our practical convention, not systematic-review completeness. If critical access or evidence is missing, keep the article a draft and explain the gap. Do not create background schedules unless requested. Do not run a skill benchmark for ordinary writing.
