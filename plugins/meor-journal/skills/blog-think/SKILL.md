# Blog Think


Read [framework choices](../../references/frameworks.md). Work from the author's actual notes and the evidence packet. Produce a compact thought brief: reader question, provisional view or unresolved tension, supporting reasons, evidence gaps, strongest alternative, and what could change the view.

Choose one dominant structure because it fits the material. Reflection can move from a real scene to its meaning and an open question. Explanation can group reasons under a bounded answer. A change-oriented piece can explore the distance between current and possible conditions and the tradeoffs. A research essay can weigh competing explanations. Do not combine every framework into a rigid mega-template.

Give 1–2 outline options only when they would produce materially different good pieces. Explain the choice briefly. A consultant framework organizes ideas; it does not validate the evidence. Use the source links in the framework guide to credit Minto and Duarte. Treat the Gibbs-inspired reflection here as an adaptation from a limited publisher record, not a full reproduction of the book.

Keep personal experience, external evidence and interpretation separate in the thought brief. Test the strongest alternative before fixing the headline. If sources weaken the thesis, revise the outline. Leave unresolved matters open when that is the honest conclusion. Do not invent scenes, dialogue, conflict or emotional transformation.

Save the thought brief and outline in the article packet, then hand off to drafting or research within the current request. An outline does not authorize publication.
