# Research brief

Question: How can Malaysian public agencies use AI to redesign services, remove duplication and reduce net government costs, and under what conditions could units or agencies be merged or dissolved?

Requested origin: Meor explicitly asked for autoresearch, beginning with Khazanah Research Institute and comparing neighbouring countries. No author-supplied personal experience was provided.

Genre candidate: researched essay. Deliverable at this stage: unpublished policy research briefing and evidence packet; no Hugo article created.

Scope: evidence available and inspected on 19 October 2026, Malaysia plus Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia and Vietnam. Historical observations are dated individually. Primary sources preferred; Thailand implementation example is a declared vendor-partner account.

Provisional view: common services and functional rationalisation may enable savings; AI uptake, exposure and time savings cannot establish agency redundancy or net fiscal savings.

What would count against it: a simpler non-AI process achieves the same benefit; supposedly duplicated services have distinct mandates or underserved recipients; integration and transition costs exceed benefits; sharing worsens access or accountability.

Reader-search question: Can AI reduce Malaysia's government costs and overlapping agencies?
Reader intent: understand concrete institutional options and credible regional comparisons.
Original contribution: extend KRI coordination findings into a conditional closure test and a three-ledger savings model.
Proposed title: Could AI Help Malaysia Build a Leaner Government?
Proposed description: Building on KRI research, a comparison of regional AI and government reform, with a practical test for duplication, agency closure and real savings.
