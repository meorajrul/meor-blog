# What Malaysia can copy from its neighbours’ MSME reforms

Research briefing for Meor · 19 October 2026 · Unpublished

## The answer

**Singapore provides the clearest completed merger of enterprise-development agencies. Indonesia provides a useful example of shared financing infrastructure with specialist institutions retained. Thailand provides a shared identity and service-marketplace model.** These are three different ways to integrate support.

The inspected evidence shows useful operating changes and some positive business outcomes. **It does not establish an audited net government saving attributable to the selected restructuring in any of these three cases.** That is an evidence gap, not proof that savings were zero. Singapore’s widely reported business cost savings should not be presented as savings in the government’s agency budget.

Malaysia can realistically copy integrated case management, simpler programme rules, common provider procurement and shared service points. The case for immediately abolishing an entire agency remains weaker than the case for transferring duplicated work. This regional comparison strengthens the functional merger proposal in the [Malaysian agency review](../../to_human/msme-agency-review.html); it does not turn its conditional INSKEN or PERNAS closure options into proven fiscal wins.

## 1. Four countries, three different integration models

“Worked” needs a specific test: was the institution or service actually integrated; did businesses obtain a usable service; did outcomes improve; and was administrative cost reduced after transition costs? A reform may pass the first two without establishing the last two.

| Country | What actually changed | Evidence of a useful result | Net government merger saving established here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Malaysia** | SME Corp coordinates a multi-agency system; SME Bank, EXIM and BPMB entered a common group in 2025 | Existing institutional capability and reported programme results; group integration has occurred | **Not established.** The earlier RM1.3m annual central scenario is a conditional model, not a saving already achieved. |
| **Singapore** | IE Singapore and SPRING became **Enterprise Singapore on 1 April 2018**; related grants were harmonised | A joined capability/internationalisation service and evidence that its grant programmes help recipient firms | **Not established.** Company cost reductions and agency expenditure are separate quantities. |
| **Thailand** | OSMEP’s SME ONE ID and Business Development Service (BDS) link identity and access to multiple providers | A common registration mechanism and an operating service catalogue | **Not established.** This is an integration comparator, not a completed agency-abolition case identified in the inspected records. |
| **Indonesia** | BRI, Pegadaian and PNM formed the **Ultra Micro holding in September 2021** | Shared SenyuM outlets, referrals and differentiated routes from very small borrowers to bank services | **Not established.** A holding is not the disappearance of the specialist entities or their costs. |

Sources: [Malaysia evidence packet](../../README.md); [Singapore government directory](https://www.sgdi.gov.sg/ministries/meti/statutory-boards/enterprisesg); [Singapore Budget 2018, Annex A-3](https://www.mof.gov.sg/docs/default-source/default-document-library/singapore-budget/budget-archives/2018/fy2018_budget_statement.pdf?sfvrsn=55b42e95_2); [OSMEP programme record](https://bds.sme.go.th/News/Detail/103); [PNM’s holding history](https://www.pnm.co.id/berita/4-tahun-holding-ultra-mikro-pnm-hadirkan-senyum-jutaan-perempuan-indonesia), [BRI’s operational report](https://bri.co.id/web/guest/promo-news-detail/1279).

Malaysia should also avoid describing itself as institutionally undeveloped. The ASEAN SME Policy Index 2024 describes its SME institutional framework as advanced. The same assessment still calls for stronger coordination and data sharing in Thailand. A respected coordinator and several portals do not, by themselves, demonstrate that duplication has been removed. This is a policy-framework assessment, not a measure of merger savings. [ASEAN/OECD/ERIA 2024, printed pp.337,429](https://www.eria.org/uploads/ASPI_2024-Full_report-upload-20Sep.pdf)

## 2. Singapore: a real merger, but the savings belong in the right ledger

IE Singapore’s internationalisation role and SPRING’s capability-development role were combined in Enterprise Singapore. The useful organisational idea is a single owner for a firm’s development journey: improving the business and entering new markets become related decisions. The 2018 budget also combined the Capability Development Grant and Global Company Partnership grant into the Enterprise Development Grant (EDG). This is stronger integration than placing separate programmes behind a common website. [Government directory](https://www.sgdi.gov.sg/ministries/meti/statutory-boards/enterprisesg), [Budget 2018, Annex A-3](https://www.mof.gov.sg/docs/default-source/default-document-library/singapore-budget/budget-archives/2018/fy2018_budget_statement.pdf?sfvrsn=55b42e95_2)

There is evidence of programme benefit after the merger. A May 2026 study uses administrative firm data, firm/year fixed effects and controls for other grants. For EDG recipients in 2019–2022, a typical grant was associated with **1.9% higher annual revenue and 1.6% higher value added**, relative to similar non-recipients. This is observational programme evaluation: time-varying selection remains a concern, and the study does not isolate the organisational merger from the grants or compare a merged agency with two continued agencies. [Ngoh and Lee, Economic Survey of Singapore Q1 2026, printed pp.42–45](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/1004/7cb36396-c58c-4bff-a50e-73a675a41bb7/FA_1Q26.pdf)

Enterprise Singapore’s January 2026 release reports **11,700 enterprises supported in 2025**, including **2,400 undertaking transformative projects**. It describes **S$12.3 billion expected additional annual company revenue** and **S$118 million expected annual company operating-cost reductions**. These are project expectations, not audited fiscal savings or a causal estimate of the 2018 merger. [2026 annual media briefing release, pp.1–2](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/-/media/esg/files/media-centre/media-releases/2026/january/mr00326_annual-media-briefing-2026.pdf)

A subsequent parliamentary reply says EDG recipients reported average annual cost reductions of **S$200,000 in 2025**. Its wording also refers to estimated savings. Neither this average nor the S$118m total is money removed from Enterprise Singapore’s own budget. Do not multiply the average by every supported company: the populations and project types differ. [Parliamentary reply, 5 August 2026](https://www.meti.gov.sg/newsroom/written-reply-to-pq-on-broader-impact-of-projects-supported-by-enterprise-development-grant-beyond-revenue-and-value-add/)

The agency’s own financial highlights put **FY2024 operating expenditure at S$326.5m**, including **S$187.3m manpower and S$139.2m other operating expenditure**, for the year ended 31 March 2025. These are agency operating highlights, not all Singapore MSME support funding. Its **S$2.5m net surplus** is an accounting result, not a measured merger saving. A current cost total cannot reveal what two unmerged agencies would have cost to deliver the same services. [Annual report 2024–2025, pp.25–26](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/-/media/esg/files/resources/publications/annual-reports/enterprisesg-annual-report-2024-2025.pdf)

**Judgement:** the institutional merger and joined programmes were delivered, and there is useful evidence for the grant mechanism. An attributable net administrative saving remains unestablished in this review. Malaysia can copy ownership of the end-to-end business journey and harmonised programme rules without claiming Singapore proved that fewer agency names produce cheaper government.

## 3. Thailand: common access can precede corporate restructuring

Thailand’s most transferable mechanism is OSMEP’s SME ONE ID linked to BDS. The programme process requires SME membership/identity verification before a development proposal and support agreement. This combines a common entry mechanism with multiple service providers. [BDS application process](https://bds.sme.go.th/Sme)

OSMEP’s December 2025 account describes more than **1,000 services from over 285 providers**, with more than **4,538 development proposals** and over **THB260m support** during the programme’s earlier operation. It reports over **THB6bn economic value** and a **THB300m FY2026 programme budget**, targeting at least **3,000 participants**. The economic-value claim is agency-reported; the article does not supply a counterfactual method. The budget and business-value claims are not government cost savings. The registered, verified, proposal and headline beneficiary counts refer to different stages and should not be interchanged. [OSMEP, 1 December 2025](https://bds.sme.go.th/News/Detail/103)

An earlier annual-report passage records **119,173 SME ONE ID registrations against a 75,500 target** in FY2023, with **THB5.418m disbursed against THB5.472m budget**. Automated access to the full report failed; these figures were inspected in indexed text at the original publisher URL and remain partial-access evidence. Registrations show use of a mechanism; they do not establish unique successful business outcomes or administrative savings. The small underspend is not a merger saving. [OSMEP annual report 2023, printed p.64](https://www.sme.go.th/uploads/file/20240919-124025_SME_Annual_lowres_final.pdf)

**Judgement:** the evidence supports an operating coordination platform, with incomplete impact verification. It does not establish that Thailand solved inter-agency overlap. For Malaysia, the practical lesson is a common case profile and a quality-controlled provider framework. Generic training can be commissioned across agencies while specialist coaches remain available. Scheme design must also protect cash-poor firms from unaffordable upfront contributions; this is a design requirement proposed here, not a measured effect of the Thai programme.

## 4. Indonesia: integrate the network while preserving different clients

The Ultra Micro holding combined BRI as parent with Pegadaian and PNM, rather than abolishing all three institutions. Its September 2021 formation is recorded in PNM’s anniversary account; full-page access failed, so that historical passage is retained as indexed-source evidence. The distinction matters: a bank, pawn-based finance and group-based empowerment serve different borrower needs. [PNM holding history](https://www.pnm.co.id/berita/4-tahun-holding-ultra-mikro-pnm-hadirkan-senyum-jutaan-perempuan-indonesia)

BRI reports **1,035 SenyuM shared outlets and 34.5m active borrowers by September 2025**. Its earlier operating description sets out a progression from PNM empowerment, through additional services from Pegadaian/BRI, to commercial microcredit. The outlet network is a concrete integration output. The borrower stock is not a count of newly included people caused by the holding, and neither number measures cash savings. Both accounts are institutional self-reports. [BRI Q3 2025 account](https://bri.co.id/web/guest/promo-news-detail/1279), [description of the customer journey](https://www.bri.co.id/web/guest/id/promo-news-detail/1076)

The inspected records describe efficiency mechanisms but do not provide a reconciled holding-specific net saving after integration and ongoing costs. BRI’s full 2025 financial statements were not accessible in this pass; no claim of inspecting them is made. Generic profit growth, funding-cost reductions or a modelled “synergy value” would not by themselves fill the gap. Bank-group gains also do not automatically reduce government appropriations.

**Judgement:** shared outlets and differentiated products were implemented; causal improvements in inclusion and net public savings remain less well established here. This is a useful service-network analogue for Malaysia, with different legal forms and governance. Test shared appointments, referrals and selected service points among TEKUN, AIM, BSN and relevant partners before assuming that one lender should absorb all their clients.

None of these monetary amounts proves that AI caused the gain. The institutional changes mainly concern service design, funding rules, data and management; AI can assist parts of the resulting workflow.

## 6. What Malaysia can realistically copy

The strongest combined model is **Singapore’s joined business journey, Thailand’s shared identity/provider framework and Indonesia’s local service network**. This is a proposed adaptation, not an empirically validated package.

| Transferable element | Malaysian application | What should actually disappear | What must be protected |
|---|---|---|---|
| One owner for a company’s development plan | SME Corp coordinates a case from capability diagnosis to financing/market referrals, with partner agencies accountable for hand-offs | Repeated generic diagnostics, duplicate document collection and inconsistent eligibility explanations | Specialist decisions, open access and a named accountable case owner |
| Harmonised capability support | Pool overlapping INSKEN/CEDAR and other generic training procurement under a common provider/quality framework | Duplicate course administration, procurement and learning-system contracts where they genuinely overlap | Industry coaches, language needs, independent access for non-bank clients and outcome verification |
| One reusable case identity | Reuse and connect suitable existing Malaysian registries before buying another portal; record which intervention each grant funds | Repeated profiles and exact duplicate claims | Lawful limited data exchange; complementary grants remain possible |
| Shared service points and referral trails | Pilot co-location/service agreements with microfinance partners in selected districts | Avoidable travel, duplicated counter administration or new leased space where a verified alternative exists | Rural access, affordable products, client choice and different risk models |
| Measured programme effectiveness | Link support records to business outcomes and compare credible treatment/comparison groups | Unsupported success claims and duplicated reporting returns | Privacy, independent challenge and evidence of distributional effects |

AI has a bounded role: identify missing documents, summarise verified case files, explain published eligibility rules and flag records for human review. Identity resolution, payment rules and the grant ledger need dependable records and explicit rules. Credit or benefit refusal requires accountable decisions and review; deploying a conversational assistant does not settle institutional design.

For the earlier INSKEN option, the regional evidence favours testing a shared capability service accessible to everyone before putting all support under a bank-linked host. PERNAS financing administration can be assessed for transfer, while franchise expertise and commercial assets require a separate plan. PUNB and TERAJU should not be abolished just because they share an economic-development objective with other bodies. A function must be shown to be duplicative, transferable and cheaper at its destination.

Malaysia already has the BPMB group integration. Finish and measure that work before adding another holding structure. For microfinance, use cooperation where ownership transfer is unnecessary. These are operational recommendations based on the compared mechanisms, not claims that the three countries have interchangeable governance or client populations.

## 7. A realistic fiscal test for Malaysia

The previous model uses **RM84.5933m of identifiable proposed 2027 cost anchors**, with assumed addressable/retired shares. Its central gross saving is **RM2.779m annually**, recurring incremental cost **RM1.5m**, and transition cost **RM4.5m**. The mature annual net is therefore about **RM1.3m**, but the three-year result is a net cost of about **RM3.4m**. Those figures are not improved by borrowing a large foreign headline that measures company gains. [Assumptions and scenarios](../../data/savings-model.json)

At the same central gross saving and recurring cost, the maximum transition cost compatible with three-year break-even is **RM1.058m**. That gives a more useful constraint for a limited shared-service trial than an unsupported target to “save billions.” It is a model-derived ceiling, not a procurement estimate.

Proposed sequence:

- **First 90 days:** reconcile contracts, staff/vacancies, programme definitions and the existing registries. Choose one support journey and establish actual cost per completed case.
- **Next six months:** test common intake, one case record and pooled generic training in a limited set of locations or cohorts. Retain a credible comparison and existing assisted access.
- **Before expansion:** verify contracts/posts actually retired; subtract migration, security, training, severance/transfer and ongoing service costs. Check errors, appeals, access and borrower outcomes.
- **After evidence:** decide whether INSKEN’s separate administration or a PERNAS function should cease. A service transfer and a legal closure are separate decisions.

The reform’s scorecard should contain both **cash paid by government** and **burden borne by businesses**. Track documents submitted repeatedly, end-to-end time, errors/appeals, completed cases, business outcomes and which groups lose access. Faster service is valuable even when no budget reduction occurs; it must be described as that benefit rather than booked as cash saved.

## 8. Three essay directions

The [three developed outlines](essay-outlines.html) offer different pieces:

- **The comparative essay:** *Can Malaysia merge its way to better MSME support?* Explain what each neighbour actually integrated and apply the distinctions to Malaysia.
- **The fiscal investigation:** *When government agencies merge, where do the savings go?* Trace every headline number to the right ledger and make a case for transparent merger accounting.
- **The practical blueprint:** *One business, one case file: rebuilding Malaysia’s MSME support for the AI age.* Describe a usable service and the conditions for retiring duplicate institutions.

The **fiscal investigation is the strongest next article**: the evidence changes the original assumption that fewer agencies necessarily produce a large saving. The comparative material can support that argument, with the blueprint as a later companion piece. These are editorial proposals for refinement, not a settled claim about Meor’s views or experiences.

## Scope and limits

This is bounded comparative desk research, not an exhaustive survey of all mergers in the four countries. Official institutional accounts are interested sources; programme evaluations are stronger for their specified questions but do not establish the effect of a whole merger. Full Thai annual-report and PNM page access remained limited; the full BRI 2025 accounts were not inspected. Missing net savings are recorded as unknown. No current agency closure has been implemented or costed from complete internal ledgers. No article, publication or author approval is recorded.
