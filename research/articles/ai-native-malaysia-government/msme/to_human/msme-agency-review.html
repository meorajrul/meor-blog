Malaysia’s MSME agencies: merge the machinery, preserve the mission

Research briefing for Meor · 19 October 2026 · Unpublished policy analysis

The decision

INSKEN is the strongest candidate for eventual closure as a standalone delivery organisation, with its coaches, programmes and contracts transferred to a shared capability service. PERNAS is a second, more conditional candidate after separating franchise development, financing and commercial assets. These are institutional design recommendations, not findings that either agency is failing overall. Their reported 2024 programme scores meet SME Corp’s threshold.

PUNB’s financing administration merits a business case for transfer to the existing development-bank infrastructure. TERAJU should concentrate on coordination and strategic facilitation while common delivery moves to shared services. The evidence does not yet justify their immediate whole-organisation closure. Retain SME Corp’s coordination and evaluation role, a regulated SME financing capability, microfinance access, MARA’s wider duties and SKM’s regulatory role.

A defensible initial savings envelope is much smaller than the headline financing allocations. A conditional model using identifiable budget lines produces approximately RM1.3 million a year in the central case at full operation, with a downside of RM0.6 million additional annual cost and an upside of RM3.6 million annual savings. With transition costs and a gradual rollout, the central case remains RM3.4 million negative over three years. These are transparent planning scenarios, not verified savings or a calibrated forecast. The implication is to test inexpensive shared delivery before committing to a costly reorganisation.

1. What the comparison can actually measure

The review covers ten core or closely related institutions: SME Corp, INSKEN, PERNAS, PUNB, SME Bank, TEKUN, AIM, MARA, TERAJU and SKM. BSN, CGC and RISDA provide additional comparisons where the same published MSME performance framework reports weaker results. They have wider mandates; CGC is a public-linked financing institution, rather than a ministry department.

Three financial quantities must stay separate:

  • Federal operating grants and service purchases: possible sources of cash savings, but a grant is not an audited breakdown of administrative overhead.
  • Development allocations, lending funds and guarantees: support delivered to businesses or financing capacity. Their full face value does not disappear as an administrative saving after a merger.
  • Entity operating expenses: a bank or company’s own accounts. Lower expenses do not automatically produce an equal reduction in federal expenditure.

The 2027 detailed estimates are the latest budget document inspected. Its operating tables compare 2026 estimates with proposed 2027 amounts; development tables identify amended 2026 amounts. Proposed spending is not actual expenditure or evidence of enacted appropriations. The 2024 programme comparison is the latest coherent cross-agency dataset inspected, not a claim about complete 2026 performance. KUSKOP 2027 estimates, printed pp.309–317

The scores cover reported MSME programmes, rather than every legal duty or the causal benefit of the whole institution. The inspected methodology describes eight outcome dimensions but does not establish an independently audited, comparable causal effect. Portfolios, populations and funding instruments differ. Beneficiary totals are not demonstrated to be unique across agencies. Dividing these flows by recipients would mix loans, guarantees, grants and training; it would not measure administrative efficiency.

There is also newer, narrower evidence. SME Bank reports FY2025 approvals of RM4.3 billion against RM3.3 billion, and average turnaround of 88 days against a 104-day charter. TEMAN TEKUN reports RM162.05 million against RM155 million for 2025. TERAJU reports RM1.228 billion against RM1 billion for 2025. These equal approximately 130.3%, 104.5% and 122.8% of the respective volume targets; the bank’s turnaround is better than its upper time limit. Approvals or financing volume are outputs, not proof of additional jobs, repayment quality or lower fiscal cost. None changes the 2024 index retroactively. SME Bank FY2025 report, pp.38–39,210–211, minister’s TEMAN statement reported by Bernama, TERAJU statement, p.2

4. What the audits reveal beneath an agency score

SME Corp, Micro Connector 2023: the detailed audit table reports 877 participants against 1,060, or 82.7% attainment. Another summary table in the same audit says 886, implying 83.6%; the discrepancy remains unresolved in this review. Three of seven listed programmes missed their participant targets. The overall BIZME assessment was nevertheless “baik.” Management reported improved delivery for the cited provider in 2024. KUSKOP internal audit 2024, printed pp.94–98, Tables 51 and54

INSKEN, IBBC 2022–2023: 314 of 1,119 participants in the reported outcome dataset offered jobs, approximately 28.1%, against a 50% outcome target: 56.1% target attainment. Other outcomes performed better, and the audit’s overall programme assessment was satisfactory. The outcome dataset is not identical to enrolment or completion counts; the survey component obtained only 89 responses from 1,000 invitations. The programme changed targets in 2024. This does not establish that current INSKEN operations fail the same KPI. Audit 2024, printed pp.103–108, Tables 57–61

SKM, SCOPE: existing distribution-centre upgrades achieved 9 against 20, or 45%; new centres achieved 30 against 20, or 150%. The audit also identified missing outcome assessment. One missed output cannot erase an exceeded output, nor does either resolve whether SKM’s regulatory function is needed. Audit 2024, SCOPE chapter and summary, printed pp.119–140

Audit follow-up matters: Table 89 reports the INSKEN and BIZME issues presented to the audit committee as completed by 14 January 2025; SCOPE still had issues in action. That administrative closure does not itself demonstrate sustained outcome recovery, but it prevents describing the earlier findings as unresolved 2026 failures. Audit 2024, printed p.161

The 2025 halal programme audit supplies a concrete shared-service case. It identified inconsistent payment procedures and reporting/verification problems across implementing agencies. A reported certification-application total of 348 was followed by a detailed reconciled count of 288 out of 351, or 82.1%, after accounting for records and duplicate outlets; the stated project outcome was at least 50% applying. PERNAS’s 24 of 27 is therefore not evidence of missing that 50% outcome target. The three withdrawals explain a missed full-cohort count, a different measure. Audit 2025, printed pp.26–35

The same audit records entrepreneurs receiving support from multiple agencies. Management explains that some support paid for different needs, such as certification, renovation and coaching. A shared beneficiary identifier should expose true duplicate claims while allowing legitimate complementary support. Multiple agency contacts are not automatically duplicate expenditure. Management responses describe new guidelines and payment coordination; these are responses, not independently verified completion in this review. Audit 2025, printed pp.30,34–35

The headline “RM0–3.6 million savings if viable” must always travel with the downside: a poorly implemented programme could cost more. Do not approve the central reorganisation on a three-year savings claim. At the central gross saving and recurring cost assumptions, the maximum transition cost compatible with three-year break-even is approximately RM1.06 million, rather than the model’s RM4.5 million. That is a useful design ceiling for a limited shared service using existing infrastructure. Proceed only if observed retirement costs and service results improve the case. A cheaper design may pay back sooner, but that needs an actual procurement and staffing plan.

The retirement assumptions require ending spend: cancelled duplicate contracts, removed purchased capacity, reduced grant requirements or vacancies that are not refilled. Redeployment, staff hours released and faster decisions are useful capacity benefits; they do not count as cash savings unless the appropriation or expenditure falls. The model excludes participant grants, loan principal, credit losses, regulatory assurance and frontline coaches. Agency ledgers must confirm that grant transfers and the ministry’s own services contain no pass-through duplication before adding the three streams.

Bank savings are a separate ledger

SME Bank’s FY2025 bank-only administrative expenses of RM6.241 million and advertising/publicity of RM12.784 million provide a small, identifiable RM19.025 million expense pool for investigation. Retiring 5–10% would give RM0.951–1.903 million gross annual cost avoidance, before implementation and ongoing costs. This is a sensitivity calculation; the categories are not proven to be wholly cash, discretionary or duplicated. It is excluded from the government savings total and must be additional to the existing BPMB integration plan. Depreciation, amortisation and the entire bank payroll have not been treated as removable cash. FY2025 financial statements, Note 25, printed p.298

Unspent balances are not recurring savings

The 2024 audit examines RM133.887 million deferred development grants at end 2022. Management says much was committed and identifies approximately RM9.97 million without commitments, with proposed reuse including 2025 operations. That old balance is neither a current recoverable amount nor an annual merger saving. Reconcile commitments, subsequent spending, permissions and refunds before booking any one-time recovery. Audit 2024, printed pp.148–157

No additional savings are booked for PERNAS, PUNB or TERAJU because the current audited operating costs and transfer obligations were not established. This is a measurement gap, not an assertion that no further saving exists.

8. The evidence that would turn this into a government decision

Within the first 90 days, the responsible ministry should assemble comparable 2024–2026 ledgers by function, staff and vacancy schedules, IT/vendor contracts, leases, funding covenants, portfolio quality and a linked but privacy-controlled beneficiary record. Obtain targets set before each measurement period, exact denominators and current audit follow-up. The unit of comparison should be a service or borrower segment, not the agency’s press-release total.

Run a limited six-month trial for common intake, document checks and halal-grant administration. Compare it with continued standalone delivery using a phased or otherwise credible comparator. Publish processing time, cash operating cost per completed case, repeat document requests, errors/appeals, inclusion by region and language, and business outcomes at 6/12/24 months. Track fraud and credit outcomes separately; faster approvals are not enough. Do not attribute all improvement to AI when rule simplification or ordinary digitisation explains it.

Before expanding, publish a reconciliation from the approved baseline to the actual contracts and posts retired; incremental platform, security and training costs; transition liabilities; and actual cash saved. Close INSKEN’s standalone structure only when its host demonstrably preserves access and the transfer produces better value. Apply a second decision to PERNAS after disentangling its non-financing assets and obligations. These are proposed gates, not completed research experiments.

9. The quadrant: relevance and performance answer different questions

The quadrant uses institutional distinctiveness horizontally and the published 2024 IAFSP result vertically. The horizontal classification is an explicit analyst judgement, not an official relevance score or a validated index:

  • Distinct institution case: a coordinating/regulatory/banking role or a specialised access network provides a concrete reason to retain an institution while improving delivery.
  • Transferable delivery case: useful services could plausibly operate in an existing host without a separate corporate structure. This does not mean the service is irrelevant.

The vertical metric is the source’s score and its 70-point meeting-target threshold. Unknown scores remain outside the four cells. Current 2025 volume results and older audit exceptions are shown as additional evidence, not silently substituted for the common 2024 score.

BSN’s wider inclusion mission, CGC’s guarantee function and RISDA’s smallholder role support placing their institutional functions on the right; their weaker programme scores still require an explanation. A change in either classification or verified performance should move an institution, rather than being forced to fit a preferred conclusion.

The main policy finding is that overlap and performance are separate tests. Good delivery can be merged; a weak programme can sit inside a necessary institution. The first practical decision is a costed INSKEN/shared-capability transfer trial, followed by PERNAS finance administration. The current public evidence supports those reviews more strongly than an immediate campaign to abolish agencies or claim billion-ringgit savings.
