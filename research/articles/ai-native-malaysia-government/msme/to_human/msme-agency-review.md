# Malaysia’s MSME agencies: merge the machinery, preserve the mission

Research briefing for Meor · 19 October 2026 · Unpublished policy analysis

## The decision

**INSKEN is the strongest candidate for eventual closure as a standalone delivery organisation, with its coaches, programmes and contracts transferred to a shared capability service. PERNAS is a second, more conditional candidate after separating franchise development, financing and commercial assets.** These are institutional design recommendations, not findings that either agency is failing overall. Their reported 2024 programme scores meet SME Corp’s threshold.

PUNB’s financing administration merits a business case for transfer to the existing development-bank infrastructure. TERAJU should concentrate on coordination and strategic facilitation while common delivery moves to shared services. The evidence does not yet justify their immediate whole-organisation closure. Retain SME Corp’s coordination and evaluation role, a regulated SME financing capability, microfinance access, MARA’s wider duties and SKM’s regulatory role.

**A defensible initial savings envelope is much smaller than the headline financing allocations.** A conditional model using identifiable budget lines produces approximately **RM1.3 million a year in the central case at full operation**, with a downside of **RM0.6 million additional annual cost** and an upside of **RM3.6 million annual savings**. With transition costs and a gradual rollout, the central case remains **RM3.4 million negative over three years**. These are transparent planning scenarios, not verified savings or a calibrated forecast. The implication is to test inexpensive shared delivery before committing to a costly reorganisation.

The earlier [AI-native government briefing](../../to_human/research-briefing.html) began with KRI’s work on fragmented AI governance and institutional capability. This extension adds agency budgets and programme evidence. KRI did not establish the named closure cases or the savings estimates proposed here. ## 1. What the comparison can actually measure

The review covers ten core or closely related institutions: SME Corp, INSKEN, PERNAS, PUNB, SME Bank, TEKUN, AIM, MARA, TERAJU and SKM. BSN, CGC and RISDA provide additional comparisons where the same published MSME performance framework reports weaker results. They have wider mandates; CGC is a public-linked financing institution, rather than a ministry department.

Three financial quantities must stay separate:

- **Federal operating grants and service purchases:** possible sources of cash savings, but a grant is not an audited breakdown of administrative overhead.
- **Development allocations, lending funds and guarantees:** support delivered to businesses or financing capacity. Their full face value does not disappear as an administrative saving after a merger.
- **Entity operating expenses:** a bank or company’s own accounts. Lower expenses do not automatically produce an equal reduction in federal expenditure.

The 2027 detailed estimates are the latest budget document inspected. Its operating tables compare 2026 estimates with **proposed 2027** amounts; development tables identify **amended 2026** amounts. Proposed spending is not actual expenditure or evidence of enacted appropriations. The 2024 programme comparison is the latest coherent cross-agency dataset inspected, not a claim about complete 2026 performance. [KUSKOP 2027 estimates, printed pp.309–317](https://belanjawan.mof.gov.my/pdf/belanjawan2027/perbelanjaan/BP.26.pdf)

## 4. What the audits reveal beneath an agency score

**SME Corp, Micro Connector 2023:** the detailed audit table reports **877 participants against 1,060**, or **82.7% attainment**. Another summary table in the same audit says 886, implying 83.6%; the discrepancy remains unresolved in this review. Three of seven listed programmes missed their participant targets. The overall BIZME assessment was nevertheless “baik.” Management reported improved delivery for the cited provider in 2024. [KUSKOP internal audit 2024, printed pp.94–98, Tables 51 and54](https://www.kuskop.gov.my/admin/files/med/image/portal/PDF/Penerbitan/FULL%20LAPORAN%20TAHUNAN%20UAD%20KUSKOP%202024.pdf)

**INSKEN, IBBC 2022–2023:** **314 of 1,119** participants in the reported outcome dataset offered jobs, approximately **28.1%**, against a **50%** outcome target: **56.1% target attainment**. Other outcomes performed better, and the audit’s overall programme assessment was satisfactory. The outcome dataset is not identical to enrolment or completion counts; the survey component obtained only 89 responses from 1,000 invitations. The programme changed targets in 2024. This does not establish that current INSKEN operations fail the same KPI. [Audit 2024, printed pp.103–108, Tables 57–61](https://www.kuskop.gov.my/admin/files/med/image/portal/PDF/Penerbitan/FULL%20LAPORAN%20TAHUNAN%20UAD%20KUSKOP%202024.pdf)

**SKM, SCOPE:** existing distribution-centre upgrades achieved **9 against 20**, or **45%**; new centres achieved **30 against 20**, or **150%**. The audit also identified missing outcome assessment. One missed output cannot erase an exceeded output, nor does either resolve whether SKM’s regulatory function is needed. [Audit 2024, SCOPE chapter and summary, printed pp.119–140](https://www.kuskop.gov.my/admin/files/med/image/portal/PDF/Penerbitan/FULL%20LAPORAN%20TAHUNAN%20UAD%20KUSKOP%202024.pdf)

**Audit follow-up matters:** Table 89 reports the INSKEN and BIZME issues presented to the audit committee as completed by **14 January 2025**; SCOPE still had issues in action. That administrative closure does not itself demonstrate sustained outcome recovery, but it prevents describing the earlier findings as unresolved 2026 failures. [Audit 2024, printed p.161](https://www.kuskop.gov.my/admin/files/med/image/portal/PDF/Penerbitan/FULL%20LAPORAN%20TAHUNAN%20UAD%20KUSKOP%202024.pdf)

**The 2025 halal programme audit supplies a concrete shared-service case.** It identified inconsistent payment procedures and reporting/verification problems across implementing agencies. A reported certification-application total of 348 was followed by a detailed reconciled count of **288 out of 351**, or **82.1%**, after accounting for records and duplicate outlets; the stated project outcome was at least **50%** applying. PERNAS’s 24 of 27 is therefore not evidence of missing that 50% outcome target. The three withdrawals explain a missed full-cohort count, a different measure. [Audit 2025, printed pp.26–35](https://www.kuskop.gov.my/admin/files/med/image/portal/PDF/Penerbitan/LAPORAN%20TAHUNAN%20UAD%20KUSKOP%202025.pdf)

The same audit records entrepreneurs receiving support from multiple agencies. Management explains that some support paid for different needs, such as certification, renovation and coaching. A shared beneficiary identifier should expose true duplicate claims while allowing legitimate complementary support. **Multiple agency contacts are not automatically duplicate expenditure.** Management responses describe new guidelines and payment coordination; these are responses, not independently verified completion in this review. [Audit 2025, printed pp.30,34–35](https://www.kuskop.gov.my/admin/files/med/image/portal/PDF/Penerbitan/LAPORAN%20TAHUNAN%20UAD%20KUSKOP%202025.pdf)

## 5. Which functions should merge

The proposed service model gives every entrepreneur one assisted entry point and one case history, while specialist institutions retain decisions that require their authority or expertise. This is an analyst proposal based on the observed mandates and audit findings.

| Function | Current overlap to investigate | Proposed destination and AI contribution | What must remain distinct |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intake, basic eligibility and referral | SME Corp, INSKEN, TEKUN, PERNAS, PUNB, MARA, TERAJU and bank advisers | A shared service accountable to KUSKOP, with SME Corp coordinating standards; AI helps identify suitable programmes and missing documents | Published rules, assisted/offline service, accountable eligibility decisions and appeals |
| Beneficiary and grant administration | Cross-agency halal assistance and other grants | Common case identifier, award ledger, approved payment rules and invoice checks; AI flags inconsistencies for review | Different legitimate interventions; exact duplicate-claim rules must be deterministic and auditable |
| Generic business training | INSKEN, CEDAR, PUNB, MARA and other providers | One provider framework and shared curriculum for bookkeeping, basic digital skills and compliance | Human industry coaches, language/access support and measured business outcomes |
| Credit paperwork, servicing and procurement | PERNAS/PUNB and development-finance infrastructure | Common administration and document processing; assess portfolio transfer separately | Credit authority, prudential controls, Shariah requirements, covenants and segment expertise |
| MSME outcome reporting | Multiple programme reports and beneficiary counts | SME Corp coordinates a common dictionary and independent evaluation access; AI assists reconciliation | Programme owners remain responsible for evidence; evaluators must be able to challenge delivery agencies |
| Rural and microfinance outreach | TEKUN, AIM, MARA and BSN touch related populations | Shared scheduling, referrals and back-office purchasing where access is preserved | Relationship-based outreach, local assistance and separate credit products until comparative evidence supports consolidation |

Start by consolidating the process and retiring a named contract, licence or vacant post. A common website added on top of unchanged systems will create another cost centre. Reuse existing government identity, data and digital infrastructure after checking their suitability. Do not build an unrestricted central pool of personal credit data; use lawful, limited exchange and logged access.

## 7. Realistic savings: a small, conditional case before a large claim

### Budget-supported delivery: prospective 2027 baseline

Use the **proposed 2027** baseline so the already proposed fall in SME Corp’s grant and ministry service purchases is not relabelled as a new merger saving. Three identifiable lines total **RM84.5933 million**. Their administrative shares are not disclosed; the following shares and retirement rates are assumptions for due diligence, not observations.

| Cost anchor, RMm | Low: addressable share × retired share | Central | High | Gross annual saving: low / central / high, RMm |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| SME Corp operating grant **42.7564** | 10% × 15% | 15% × 20% | 20% × 25% | 0.641 / 1.283 / 2.138 |
| INSKEN operating grant **12.0009** | 20% × 20% | 27.5% × 25% | 35% × 30% | 0.480 / 0.825 / 1.260 |
| KUSKOP management programme’s supplies/services **29.8360** | 10% × 10% | 15% × 15% | 20% × 20% | 0.298 / 0.671 / 1.193 |
| **Total gross annual cost removed** | | | | **1.420 / 2.779 / 4.591** |
| Additional recurring shared-service cost | | | | **2.000 / 1.500 / 1.000** |
| **Net annual savings at full operation** | | | | **−0.580 / 1.279 / 3.591** |
| One-time transition cost | | | | **6.000 / 4.500 / 3.000** |
| **Three-year net savings with rollout** | | | | **−9.161 / −3.442 / 3.183** |

Formula: anchor × addressable share × genuinely retired share. Annual net = gross savings less incremental operation. Three-year calculation assumes **25%,75%,100%** of gross savings in years 1–3, full annual incremental cost, and transition cost once. All amounts are nominal; no discounting or inflation adjustment. The central case reaches cumulative break-even at approximately **5.7 years**, assuming benefits and costs remain stable after year 3. [Baseline: KUSKOP 2027 B.26, printed pp.314–316](https://belanjawan.mof.gov.my/pdf/belanjawan2027/perbelanjaan/BP.26.pdf)

The headline “RM0–3.6 million savings if viable” must always travel with the downside: a poorly implemented programme could cost more. **Do not approve the central reorganisation on a three-year savings claim.** At the central gross saving and recurring cost assumptions, the maximum transition cost compatible with three-year break-even is approximately **RM1.06 million**, rather than the model’s RM4.5 million. That is a useful design ceiling for a limited shared service using existing infrastructure. Proceed only if observed retirement costs and service results improve the case. A cheaper design may pay back sooner, but that needs an actual procurement and staffing plan.

The retirement assumptions require ending spend: cancelled duplicate contracts, removed purchased capacity, reduced grant requirements or vacancies that are not refilled. Redeployment, staff hours released and faster decisions are useful capacity benefits; they do not count as cash savings unless the appropriation or expenditure falls. The model excludes participant grants, loan principal, credit losses, regulatory assurance and frontline coaches. Agency ledgers must confirm that grant transfers and the ministry’s own services contain no pass-through duplication before adding the three streams.

### Bank savings are a separate ledger

SME Bank’s FY2025 bank-only **administrative expenses of RM6.241 million** and **advertising/publicity of RM12.784 million** provide a small, identifiable **RM19.025 million** expense pool for investigation. Retiring **5–10%** would give **RM0.951–1.903 million gross annual cost avoidance**, before implementation and ongoing costs. This is a sensitivity calculation; the categories are not proven to be wholly cash, discretionary or duplicated. It is excluded from the government savings total and must be additional to the existing BPMB integration plan. Depreciation, amortisation and the entire bank payroll have not been treated as removable cash. [FY2025 financial statements, Note 25, printed p.298](https://www.smebank.com.my/documents/d/guest/sme-bank_iar-2025-final)

### Unspent balances are not recurring savings

The 2024 audit examines **RM133.887 million deferred development grants at end 2022**. Management says much was committed and identifies approximately **RM9.97 million** without commitments, with proposed reuse including 2025 operations. That old balance is neither a current recoverable amount nor an annual merger saving. Reconcile commitments, subsequent spending, permissions and refunds before booking any one-time recovery. [Audit 2024, printed pp.148–157](https://www.kuskop.gov.my/admin/files/med/image/portal/PDF/Penerbitan/FULL%20LAPORAN%20TAHUNAN%20UAD%20KUSKOP%202024.pdf)

No additional savings are booked for PERNAS, PUNB or TERAJU because the current audited operating costs and transfer obligations were not established. This is a measurement gap, not an assertion that no further saving exists.

## 8. The evidence that would turn this into a government decision

Within the first 90 days, the responsible ministry should assemble comparable 2024–2026 ledgers by function, staff and vacancy schedules, IT/vendor contracts, leases, funding covenants, portfolio quality and a linked but privacy-controlled beneficiary record. Obtain targets set before each measurement period, exact denominators and current audit follow-up. The unit of comparison should be a service or borrower segment, not the agency’s press-release total.

Run a limited six-month trial for common intake, document checks and halal-grant administration. Compare it with continued standalone delivery using a phased or otherwise credible comparator. Publish processing time, cash operating cost per completed case, repeat document requests, errors/appeals, inclusion by region and language, and business outcomes at 6/12/24 months. Track fraud and credit outcomes separately; faster approvals are not enough. Do not attribute all improvement to AI when rule simplification or ordinary digitisation explains it.

Before expanding, publish a reconciliation from the approved baseline to the actual contracts and posts retired; incremental platform, security and training costs; transition liabilities; and actual cash saved. Close INSKEN’s standalone structure only when its host demonstrably preserves access and the transfer produces better value. Apply a second decision to PERNAS after disentangling its non-financing assets and obligations. These are proposed gates, not completed research experiments.

## 9. The quadrant: relevance and performance answer different questions

The quadrant uses **institutional distinctiveness** horizontally and the **published 2024 IAFSP result** vertically. The horizontal classification is an explicit analyst judgement, not an official relevance score or a validated index:

- **Distinct institution case:** a coordinating/regulatory/banking role or a specialised access network provides a concrete reason to retain an institution while improving delivery.
- **Transferable delivery case:** useful services could plausibly operate in an existing host without a separate corporate structure. This does not mean the service is irrelevant.

The vertical metric is the source’s score and its **70-point meeting-target threshold**. Unknown scores remain outside the four cells. Current 2025 volume results and older audit exceptions are shown as additional evidence, not silently substituted for the common 2024 score.

| | Transferable delivery: test merger | Distinct institution case: retain role |
|---|---|---|
| **Meets published framework: ≥70** | **INSKEN 95.4; PERNAS 87.1; PUNB 99.5.** Merge administration or test host transfer even though reported performance passes. | **SME Corp 99.5; SME Bank 70.3; TEKUN 87.2; AIM 97.2; MARA 99.1.** Keep the distinct role and remove duplicated work. |
| **Below published framework: <70** | **No core institution established in this cell.** It would be misleading to populate it to manufacture a closure list. | **BSN 41.8; CGC 66.7; RISDA 67.4.** Investigate the measured MSME programmes and redesign weak delivery; this is not evidence to abolish the whole bank, guarantee institution or smallholder authority. |
| **Unknown or not comparable** | **TERAJU:** mixed coordination and delivery; separate the two functions before classifying the whole institution. | **SKM:** no separate comparable score established here; its regulatory role remains distinct. |

Scores: [SMEIPA pp.41,48,56,66–67](https://anyflip.com/wtvjw/huat/basic), [agency detail](https://anyflip.com/wtvjw/huat/basic/51-100). Classification rationale and underlying records are retained in the [agency comparison data](../data/agencies.json). BSN’s wider inclusion mission, CGC’s guarantee function and RISDA’s smallholder role support placing their institutional functions on the right; their weaker programme scores still require an explanation. A change in either classification or verified performance should move an institution, rather than being forced to fit a preferred conclusion.

**The main policy finding is that overlap and performance are separate tests.** Good delivery can be merged; a weak programme can sit inside a necessary institution. The first practical decision is a costed INSKEN/shared-capability transfer trial, followed by PERNAS finance administration. The current public evidence supports those reviews more strongly than an immediate campaign to abolish agencies or claim billion-ringgit savings.
