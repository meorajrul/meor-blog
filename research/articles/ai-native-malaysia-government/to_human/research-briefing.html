The answer the evidence supports

Malaysia can use AI as part of a programme to remove duplicated work and improve public services. Closing an agency requires a further case: its useful functions must have a viable home, its legal duties must be addressed, and the change must deliver better value after transition costs.

The strongest proposition to develop is: make each public service justify its procedures and costs, then make each agency justify the functions that remain. AI can help expose duplication and reduce some processing work. Authority, incentives and budget decisions determine whether those improvements become a smaller administrative footprint.

This briefing starts with KRI, compares Malaysia with Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia and Vietnam, and proposes a testable reform programme. These countries offer different comparisons: shared AI products, pilots, digital integration and machinery-of-government reform. They are not one comparable experiment.

1. What Khazanah Research Institute has already established

KRI's January 2025 report, AI Governance in Malaysia: Risks, Challenges and Pathways Forward, is directly relevant. Jun-E Tan, Rachel Gong, Khoo Wei Yang and Nik Syafiah Anis Nik Sharifulden drew on 21 interviews conducted in March–August 2024, a 36-person roundtable, and desk research. Its findings include fragmented coordination and insufficient governance capacity. It recommends coordinating existing actors, stronger data governance, capability development and independent oversight. See printed pp. 3–4, 26–27, 35 and 39–41. KRI report and original download

The related March 2025 commentary argues for regulation that can adapt to different sectors and risks. Finding the Right Balance

What we build on: coordination should become a practical review of duplicated processes, spending and institutional responsibilities.

What this adds: an agency-review decision rule, a Malaysian case study, a comparison that separates AI from wider digital reform, and an accounting test for savings. These are proposals from this research, not KRI recommendations to disband named agencies.

KRI's qualitative design helps identify governance problems; it does not estimate fiscal effects or establish which agencies should close. The targeted KRI searches found related governance work, but did not establish an evaluated AI-driven agency-closure programme. That is a search finding, not proof that no such publication exists.

2. Malaysia is already adopting AI; the harder question is institutional change

The Ministry of Digital reported 373,346 active AI@Work users as of December 2025. This establishes a reported adoption footprint. The release does not provide a controlled productivity estimate, a reproducible definition of activity, or audited fiscal savings. It cannot be read as evidence that those users' positions are redundant. 6 January 2026 ministry release

The World Bank's 2026 GovTech publication summary draws on a 2025 skills survey with more than 16,500 civil servants across 28 ministries. It identifies uneven implementation, data-sharing practices, advanced skills and digital leadership as constraints despite strong foundations. Only the publisher's substantive overview was inspected here; the full survey methods and report were not appraised. Building Malaysia's Future Government

Two developments update KRI's institutional snapshot:

  • MD2030, announced in June 2026, includes a RM4.5 billion public-sector digitalisation savings target towards 2030. The announcement does not establish realised savings, an annual versus cumulative basis, net costs, or AI's individual contribution. MD2030 announcement
  • AI Malaysia Berhad was announced in July 2026 as the institutionalisation of NAIO. It should not be counted as an additional separate NAIO-like body solely because older documents use the previous name. AI Malaysia announcement

JDN states that the Data Sharing Act 2025 came into force on 28 April 2025 and applies to the Federal Government. This provides an institutional starting point for sharing; this briefing does not infer unrestricted access or automatic coverage of every state, local, statutory or private data holder. The precise scope and powers need checking against the Act for each proposed transfer. JDN explanation of Act 864

Agency rationalisation also predates this proposal. A February 2025 statement carried by MOF described a federal statutory-body rationalisation process addressing overlapping functions. It records a policy process, not completed closures or savings attributable to AI. MOF statement

The differences in state structure, geography, budgets and administrative systems limit direct transfer. These selected cases cannot establish that Malaysia trails all its neighbours in actual AI use.

4. What “AI-native” should mean for a public agency

Working definition proposed here: an agency designs services, records, workflows, roles and controls to use AI where it improves verified outcomes, while retaining accountable decision-making. Giving every officer a chatbot meets only part of that definition.

The design should include:

  1. A named owner for the complete citizen or business journey across agency boundaries.
  2. Reusable identity, data exchange, payments, case management and AI services.
  3. Machine-readable rules and authoritative records with clear access permissions.
  4. AI for suitable tasks: document classification, information retrieval, drafting, translation and anomaly triage.
  5. Deterministic software for fixed calculations and rules; removal of unnecessary requirements before automation.
  6. Staff able to investigate exceptions, challenge outputs and improve the service.
  7. Published performance measures, accountable review and a route to contest consequential decisions.

This is a proposed model, not an empirical maturity classification. OECD's government-AI review identifies governance, data, infrastructure, skills, investment, procurement and partnerships as enabling conditions; it also describes accuracy, accountability and exclusion risks. Initial recommendation: consolidate common intake, document checks, reporting, purchasing and software where the evidence permits. Retain specialist regulatory, technical and frontline capability unless a review establishes a better arrangement. For a whole-agency closure, require an agency-specific case rather than inferring failure from its name or low AI uptake.

A practical first case: MSME support

The World Bank's 2022 SME programme review maps more than 270 distinct programmes available during 2016–2019, using SMEIPA data and other programme information. This is a historical portfolio, not today's programme count. Its analysis provides a basis for examining fragmentation. SME programme efficiency review, printed p. 37 onward

An earlier World Bank review identifies coordination constraints and potential conflicts when SME Corp both coordinates and implements programmes. It does not establish current legal arrangements or current agency failure. SME Masterplan review, printed pp. 43 and 50

SME Corp's SMEIPA 2025 description confirms an existing coordination, monitoring and evaluation mechanism. That page was available through indexed publisher text; a direct fetch returned 403. Use it as a starting inventory mechanism, not a source of uninspected current programme totals. SMEIPA 2025 description

Proposed pilot: select a bounded set of support schemes with comparable applicant needs. Build one application profile, a verified scheme catalogue, reusable document checks and common status tracking. AI can suggest possible matches and flag potentially duplicated submissions. Scheme rules and accountable officers determine eligibility.

Compare SME Corp and MDEC programme processes where relevant; add other agencies only after inspecting their mandates and programme records. Do not assume different lending, export, inclusion or regional-development programmes are interchangeable.

Before any closure recommendation, review beneficiary populations, statutory duties, actual outcomes, expenditure and commitments, regional access, and the full cost of migration. A programme can be merged or stopped while its host agency continues useful work.

6. When would disbanding an agency be justified?

Use four distinct options, in order of the evidence required:

DecisionWhat must be demonstrated
Simplify or automate a taskThe task is necessary and the revised process performs better than the existing and simpler non-AI alternatives
Share a serviceCommon delivery can meet each participant's requirements at lower total cost or better quality
Merge or close a programme/unitIts purpose duplicates another, is obsolete, or produces inadequate additional value; beneficiaries and obligations have a workable destination
Dissolve an agencyIts remaining useful functions can be transferred or lawfully ended; liabilities, staff, assets, records, contracts and service access are addressed; the whole case delivers better value

Proposed closure test: clear functional justification + viable service replacement + lawful transition + independently assessed net value.

Failure to adopt AI is a reason to investigate leadership, capability and service design. It is not by itself a dissolution criterion. Equally, AI branding should not protect an agency whose purpose is obsolete or whose outcomes remain poor after a fair review.

The UK National Audit Office's 2026 synthesis is a useful countercheck: reorganisations can yield benefits, but unclear goals, untracked costs, disruption and underestimated integration work can undermine them. Its lessons are contextual evidence, not a prediction of Malaysia's outcome. NAO: Reorganising government?

7. What would count as real government savings?

Keep three ledgers:

  • Fiscal savings: actual reductions against a documented spending baseline.
  • Released capacity or avoided future cost: staff time redeployed, backlogs cleared, or growth in staffing avoided against an explicit counterfactual.
  • Public benefit: reduced waiting, fewer trips and forms, improved access and fewer errors.

All can matter. They should not be added together and labelled cash saved. The UK's benefits framework distinguishes productivity from potential cost reductions, calls for sensitivity analysis and warns against double-counting. It supplies appraisal guidance, not Malaysian effect sizes. Digital and Data Benefits framework.

Proposed accounting identity

Net fiscal saving over a stated period = spending under the credible business-as-usual counterfactual minus all spending under the reform.

Include AI licences, infrastructure, data preparation, verification, security, audit, training, parallel running, contract exit charges, workforce transition and retained obligations. Include costs transferred to another agency. Keep citizen compliance costs visible separately.

A retired contract can produce cash savings. Time saved by staff who remain employed is released capacity until it enables a verifiable spending reduction. Lower headcount does not establish savings if outsourcing or system costs rise.

8. A testable reform programme

This is a proposed timetable for a bounded pilot, not an existing government commitment. Full agency integration may take several years.

PeriodWorkEvidence required to proceed
First 90 daysSelect one service cluster; inventory mandates, systems, contracts, costs and citizen journeys; establish baselineNamed owner, data permissions, comparable cases, service-access plan and explicit alternatives
Months 4–6Test simplified existing process, shared service without AI, and shared service with AICase-mix-adjusted cost and quality, total completion time, rework, staff verification time and subgroup access
Months 7–12Expand successful components; migrate carefully; retire validated duplicatesObservable service performance and actual contracts or spending removed
ThereafterDecide which units/programmes to retain, merge, redesign or sunset; consider agency dissolution separatelyIndependent whole-case assessment, lawful transition and sustained performance

Where feasible, randomise suitable low-risk cases or phase rollout across comparable offices. Record the design before testing. No trials have been conducted in this research.

Primary pilot outcome: total cost per correctly completed case, including review and rework. Also measure median and slow-case completion times, appeal reversals, access by geography and language, assisted-channel demand, data incidents and staff workload.

Use a non-AI comparator so that gains from simpler rules or shared infrastructure are not credited to AI. Define acceptable quality and access thresholds before seeing results, tailored to the consequences of error. Do not invent one universal percentage.

Budget ownership matters: a shared service needs authority to stop duplicate renewals once migration succeeds. Staff transition needs credible redeployment, training and attrition plans. Capability needed for audit, enforcement and frontline service may need more investment.

These are exploratory evidence assessments. They are not preregistered experiments, a systematic review, or independent performance audits.

Two final targeted checks examined (1) current institutions and the superseding readiness-index version and (2) merger costs and benefit accounting. They resolved wording and comparison risks but did not establish a defensible named closure or a Malaysian net-savings estimate. Conclude this desk-research phase; deepen with agency-level records before making those stronger claims.

10. The journal angle worth developing

Working title: Could AI Help Malaysia Build a Leaner Government?

Central question: If AI lets government share more work, which procedures, programmes and institutions still earn their cost?

Provisional argument: Malaysia should connect AI adoption to a review of how services are organised. Shared capabilities and measured outcomes can expose unnecessary duplication. Agency closure should follow evidence that useful services survive and net value improves.

Original contribution: extend KRI's coordination diagnosis into a concrete functional-review and savings framework, using MSME support as an investigable case.

Evidence still needed for a stronger essay: current programme-level spending and outcomes, agency statutes and audited accounts, contracts and workforce obligations, documented beneficiary journeys, evaluations of actual AI use, and responses from affected institutions and service users. These could support an agency-specific follow-up. They are not needed to explain the conditional framework honestly.
