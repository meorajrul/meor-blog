# Could AI Help Malaysia Build a Leaner Government?

Research briefing prepared for Meor · 19 October 2026 · Asia/Kuala_Lumpur
Status: exploratory policy research; unpublished; no author approval recorded.

## The answer the evidence supports

Malaysia can use AI as part of a programme to remove duplicated work and improve public services. Closing an agency requires a further case: its useful functions must have a viable home, its legal duties must be addressed, and the change must deliver better value after transition costs.

The strongest proposition to develop is: **make each public service justify its procedures and costs, then make each agency justify the functions that remain.** AI can help expose duplication and reduce some processing work. Authority, incentives and budget decisions determine whether those improvements become a smaller administrative footprint.

This briefing starts with KRI, compares Malaysia with Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia and Vietnam, and proposes a testable reform programme. These countries offer different comparisons: shared AI products, pilots, digital integration and machinery-of-government reform. They are not one comparable experiment.

## 1. What Khazanah Research Institute has already established

KRI's January 2025 report, *AI Governance in Malaysia: Risks, Challenges and Pathways Forward*, is directly relevant. Jun-E Tan, Rachel Gong, Khoo Wei Yang and Nik Syafiah Anis Nik Sharifulden drew on 21 interviews conducted in March–August 2024, a 36-person roundtable, and desk research. Its findings include fragmented coordination and insufficient governance capacity. It recommends coordinating existing actors, stronger data governance, capability development and independent oversight. See printed pp. 3–4, 26–27, 35 and 39–41. [KRI report and original download](https://www.krinstitute.org/publications/AI_Governance_in_Malaysia-_Risks_Challenges_and_Pathways_Forward)

The related March 2025 commentary argues for regulation that can adapt to different sectors and risks. [Finding the Right Balance](https://www.krinstitute.org/publications/finding-the-right-balance-governing-ai-in-a-rapidly-evolving-future)

**What we build on:** coordination should become a practical review of duplicated processes, spending and institutional responsibilities.

**What this adds:** an agency-review decision rule, a Malaysian case study, a comparison that separates AI from wider digital reform, and an accounting test for savings. These are proposals from this research, not KRI recommendations to disband named agencies.

KRI's qualitative design helps identify governance problems; it does not estimate fiscal effects or establish which agencies should close. The targeted KRI searches found related governance work, but did not establish an evaluated AI-driven agency-closure programme. That is a search finding, not proof that no such publication exists.

## 2. Malaysia is already adopting AI; the harder question is institutional change

The Ministry of Digital reported 373,346 active AI@Work users as of December 2025. This establishes a reported adoption footprint. The release does not provide a controlled productivity estimate, a reproducible definition of activity, or audited fiscal savings. It cannot be read as evidence that those users' positions are redundant. [6 January 2026 ministry release](https://www.digital.gov.my/en-GB/siaran/Revolusi-Pendigitalan-Sektor-Awam)

The World Bank's 2026 GovTech publication summary draws on a 2025 skills survey with more than 16,500 civil servants across 28 ministries. It identifies uneven implementation, data-sharing practices, advanced skills and digital leadership as constraints despite strong foundations. Only the publisher's substantive overview was inspected here; the full survey methods and report were not appraised. [Building Malaysia's Future Government](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/malaysia/publication/building-malaysia-s-future-government-unlocking-public-sector-productivity-with-govtech)

Two developments update KRI's institutional snapshot:

- MD2030, announced in June 2026, includes a **RM4.5 billion public-sector digitalisation savings target towards 2030**. The announcement does not establish realised savings, an annual versus cumulative basis, net costs, or AI's individual contribution. [MD2030 announcement](https://www.digital.gov.my/en-GB/siaran/Kementerian-Digital-Terajui-Transformasi-AI-Negara)
- AI Malaysia Berhad was announced in July 2026 as the institutionalisation of NAIO. It should not be counted as an additional separate NAIO-like body solely because older documents use the previous name. [AI Malaysia announcement](https://www.digital.gov.my/en-GB/siaran/AI-Malaysia-Pemacu-Utama-Menuju-Negara-AI-2030)

JDN states that the Data Sharing Act 2025 came into force on 28 April 2025 and applies to the Federal Government. This provides an institutional starting point for sharing; this briefing does not infer unrestricted access or automatic coverage of every state, local, statutory or private data holder. The precise scope and powers need checking against the Act for each proposed transfer. [JDN explanation of Act 864](https://www.jdn.gov.my/en/data-sharing-act-2025-act-864/)

Agency rationalisation also predates this proposal. A February 2025 statement carried by MOF described a federal statutory-body rationalisation process addressing overlapping functions. It records a policy process, not completed closures or savings attributable to AI. [MOF statement](https://www.mof.gov.my/portal/en/news/press-citations/proposal-to-streamline-govt-agencies-to-be-presented-to-cabinet-pm-anwar)

### What each country actually demonstrates

| Country | Inspected evidence | Useful lesson for Malaysia | Limit of the comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| Malaysia | AI@Work reported active use; 2026 institutional and digitalisation plans | Move from tool uptake to measured service redesign and retired costs | Adoption and targets do not establish cash savings |
| Singapore | GovTech describes shared Pair, VICA and WOGAA products; Pair's change log records iterative releases and an evaluation suite | Reuse common tools and evaluate them in public-service contexts | Operator descriptions do not prove AI-caused agency closures |
| Thailand | DGA–Microsoft training and a 2025 hackathon, including land-document and procurement projects | Train teams around actual workflows and develop prototypes | Vendor partner reporting; pilots do not establish nationwide operational savings |
| Indonesia | INA Digital integration programme and limited service launches in 2024 | Integrate duplicated applications and citizen journeys before adding more systems | Historical integration evidence, not a verified 2026 AI adoption rate |
| Vietnam | 2025 restructuring created 14 ministries plus three ministerial-level agencies; a separate AI strategy includes public-administration aims | Institutional reform needs decisions about authority as well as technology | The records do not show that AI caused the restructuring or delivered net savings |

Indonesia's ministry reported roughly 27,000 government applications at the 2024 launch and a request to stop creating new ones. That is an attributed historical count, not a verified current inventory. Vietnam's reorganisation is likewise described as a dated event, rather than a claim about every current institution.

**Inference:** Singapore is the most useful operational reference in this set for shared government AI capabilities. Indonesia is a useful warning about application fragmentation. Vietnam provides a structural-reform comparison. Malaysia should borrow the relevant mechanisms and test local results.

The differences in state structure, geography, budgets and administrative systems limit direct transfer. These selected cases cannot establish that Malaysia trails all its neighbours in actual AI use.

## 4. What “AI-native” should mean for a public agency

Working definition proposed here: **an agency designs services, records, workflows, roles and controls to use AI where it improves verified outcomes, while retaining accountable decision-making.** Giving every officer a chatbot meets only part of that definition.

The design should include:

1. A named owner for the complete citizen or business journey across agency boundaries.
2. Reusable identity, data exchange, payments, case management and AI services.
3. Machine-readable rules and authoritative records with clear access permissions.
4. AI for suitable tasks: document classification, information retrieval, drafting, translation and anomaly triage.
5. Deterministic software for fixed calculations and rules; removal of unnecessary requirements before automation.
6. Staff able to investigate exceptions, challenge outputs and improve the service.
7. Published performance measures, accountable review and a route to contest consequential decisions.

This is a proposed model, not an empirical maturity classification. OECD's government-AI review identifies governance, data, infrastructure, skills, investment, procurement and partnerships as enabling conditions; it also describes accuracy, accountability and exclusion risks. [OECD government-AI review](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/governing-with-artificial-intelligence_795de142-en.html)

## 5. Which Malaysian organisations should lead, and which functions merit review?

The June MD2030 announcement assigns different roles to JDN/GovTech Malaysia, MDEC, MyDIGITAL and NAIO, subsequently institutionalised as AI Malaysia. Similar digital vocabulary is not sufficient evidence that these organisations have identical mandates. [Published implementation roles](https://www.digital.gov.my/en-GB/siaran/Kementerian-Digital-Terajui-Transformasi-AI-Negara); [July institutional update](https://www.digital.gov.my/en-GB/siaran/AI-Malaysia-Pemacu-Utama-Menuju-Negara-AI-2030).

The following is a proposed division of responsibility, using those roles as a starting point. It is not a finding that every role below is already legally assigned.

| Organisation or group | Proposed role in reform | Question for a functional review |
|---|---|---|
| Chief Secretary and central administration | Sponsor cross-agency service ownership and resolve mandate disputes | Which decisions require central direction rather than another committee? |
| MOF | Approve business cases, control duplicate purchases and verify net savings | Which contracts, premises or appropriations will actually cease? |
| JPA | Redesign jobs, redeploy staff, retain specialist skills and plan attrition | Can capacity move to unmet needs, and when can spending genuinely fall? |
| JDN / GovTech Malaysia | Operate reusable government platforms and technical standards | Which separate systems can be retired after migration? |
| AI Malaysia | Set AI policy, assurance and common evaluation expectations | Are adoption programmes or assurance functions duplicated elsewhere? |
| MyDIGITAL | Track delivery and dependencies through the existing MD2030 mechanism | Can reporting and programme management be simplified? |
| MDEC | Support business digital adoption rather than duplicate government infrastructure | Which MSME support delivery processes can be shared? |
| SME Corp and participating programme agencies | Provide a starting inventory for the MSME-support case | Which schemes have genuinely equivalent purposes and recipients? |

**Initial recommendation:** consolidate common intake, document checks, reporting, purchasing and software where the evidence permits. Retain specialist regulatory, technical and frontline capability unless a review establishes a better arrangement. For a whole-agency closure, require an agency-specific case rather than inferring failure from its name or low AI uptake.

### A practical first case: MSME support

The World Bank's 2022 SME programme review maps more than 270 distinct programmes available during **2016–2019**, using SMEIPA data and other programme information. This is a historical portfolio, not today's programme count. Its analysis provides a basis for examining fragmentation. [SME programme efficiency review, printed p. 37 onward](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099255003152238688/pdf/P17014606709a70f50856d0799328fb7040.pdf)

An earlier World Bank review identifies coordination constraints and potential conflicts when SME Corp both coordinates and implements programmes. It does not establish current legal arrangements or current agency failure. [SME Masterplan review, printed pp. 43 and 50](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/504361583989615623/pdf/Malaysia-s-Experience-with-the-Small-and-Medium-Sized-Enterprises-Masterplan-Lessons-Learned.pdf)

SME Corp's SMEIPA 2025 description confirms an existing coordination, monitoring and evaluation mechanism. That page was available through indexed publisher text; a direct fetch returned 403. Use it as a starting inventory mechanism, not a source of uninspected current programme totals. [SMEIPA 2025 description](https://smecorp.gov.my/index.php/en/popular-articles/9-uncategorised/5133-sme-integrated-plan-of-action-smeipa-2025)

**Proposed pilot:** select a bounded set of support schemes with comparable applicant needs. Build one application profile, a verified scheme catalogue, reusable document checks and common status tracking. AI can suggest possible matches and flag potentially duplicated submissions. Scheme rules and accountable officers determine eligibility.

Compare SME Corp and MDEC programme processes where relevant; add other agencies only after inspecting their mandates and programme records. Do not assume different lending, export, inclusion or regional-development programmes are interchangeable.

Before any closure recommendation, review beneficiary populations, statutory duties, actual outcomes, expenditure and commitments, regional access, and the full cost of migration. A programme can be merged or stopped while its host agency continues useful work.

## 6. When would disbanding an agency be justified?

Use four distinct options, in order of the evidence required:

| Decision | What must be demonstrated |
|---|---|
| Simplify or automate a task | The task is necessary and the revised process performs better than the existing and simpler non-AI alternatives |
| Share a service | Common delivery can meet each participant's requirements at lower total cost or better quality |
| Merge or close a programme/unit | Its purpose duplicates another, is obsolete, or produces inadequate additional value; beneficiaries and obligations have a workable destination |
| Dissolve an agency | Its remaining useful functions can be transferred or lawfully ended; liabilities, staff, assets, records, contracts and service access are addressed; the whole case delivers better value |

Proposed closure test: **clear functional justification + viable service replacement + lawful transition + independently assessed net value.**

Failure to adopt AI is a reason to investigate leadership, capability and service design. It is not by itself a dissolution criterion. Equally, AI branding should not protect an agency whose purpose is obsolete or whose outcomes remain poor after a fair review.

The UK National Audit Office's 2026 synthesis is a useful countercheck: reorganisations can yield benefits, but unclear goals, untracked costs, disruption and underestimated integration work can undermine them. Its lessons are contextual evidence, not a prediction of Malaysia's outcome. [NAO: Reorganising government?](https://www.nao.org.uk/insights/reorganising-government-six-things-to-consider/)

## 7. What would count as real government savings?

Keep three ledgers:

- **Fiscal savings:** actual reductions against a documented spending baseline.
- **Released capacity or avoided future cost:** staff time redeployed, backlogs cleared, or growth in staffing avoided against an explicit counterfactual.
- **Public benefit:** reduced waiting, fewer trips and forms, improved access and fewer errors.

All can matter. They should not be added together and labelled cash saved. The UK's benefits framework distinguishes productivity from potential cost reductions, calls for sensitivity analysis and warns against double-counting. It supplies appraisal guidance, not Malaysian effect sizes. [Digital and Data Benefits framework](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/digital-and-data-benefits-framework/digital-and-data-benefits-framework).

### Proposed accounting identity

**Net fiscal saving over a stated period = spending under the credible business-as-usual counterfactual minus all spending under the reform.**

Include AI licences, infrastructure, data preparation, verification, security, audit, training, parallel running, contract exit charges, workforce transition and retained obligations. Include costs transferred to another agency. Keep citizen compliance costs visible separately.

A retired contract can produce cash savings. Time saved by staff who remain employed is released capacity until it enables a verifiable spending reduction. Lower headcount does not establish savings if outsourcing or system costs rise.

### Illustrative arithmetic only: no Malaysian agency estimate

All figures below are deliberately hypothetical, in RM million.

| Item | First year | Later annual steady state |
|---|---:|---:|
| Duplicated spending actually removed | 12 | 12 |
| Added recurring service and assurance costs | -4 | -4 |
| One-off migration and transition costs | -10 | 0 |
| Net fiscal saving | **-2** | **8** |

If only RM5 million is removed, the corresponding results are **-RM9 million** in year one and **RM1 million** later. This sensitivity shows why implementation and cost retirement matter. It does not forecast savings, price a vendor contract, or imply that an agency can remove these amounts.

If no spending is retired, recurring costs rise by RM4 million in this example even if officers complete tasks faster. A service improvement could still justify that cost, but it must be described honestly.

## 8. A testable reform programme

This is a proposed timetable for a bounded pilot, not an existing government commitment. Full agency integration may take several years.

| Period | Work | Evidence required to proceed |
|---|---|---|
| First 90 days | Select one service cluster; inventory mandates, systems, contracts, costs and citizen journeys; establish baseline | Named owner, data permissions, comparable cases, service-access plan and explicit alternatives |
| Months 4–6 | Test simplified existing process, shared service without AI, and shared service with AI | Case-mix-adjusted cost and quality, total completion time, rework, staff verification time and subgroup access |
| Months 7–12 | Expand successful components; migrate carefully; retire validated duplicates | Observable service performance and actual contracts or spending removed |
| Thereafter | Decide which units/programmes to retain, merge, redesign or sunset; consider agency dissolution separately | Independent whole-case assessment, lawful transition and sustained performance |

Where feasible, randomise suitable low-risk cases or phase rollout across comparable offices. Record the design before testing. No trials have been conducted in this research.

Primary pilot outcome: **total cost per correctly completed case**, including review and rework. Also measure median and slow-case completion times, appeal reversals, access by geography and language, assisted-channel demand, data incidents and staff workload.

Use a non-AI comparator so that gains from simpler rules or shared infrastructure are not credited to AI. Define acceptable quality and access thresholds before seeing results, tailored to the consequences of error. Do not invent one universal percentage.

Budget ownership matters: a shared service needs authority to stop duplicate renewals once migration succeeds. Staff transition needs credible redeployment, training and attrition plans. Capability needed for audit, enforcement and frontline service may need more investment.

## 9. What changed during the research loops

| Proposition | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|
| KRI provides a foundation for this topic | Its governance work directly supports examining coordination and capacity | Supported within qualitative scope |
| Malaysia mainly needs to start using AI | Existing adoption and 2026 plans make that framing incomplete | Reframed toward service and institutional change |
| Regional AI adoption proves agencies can be closed cheaply | Sources show different kinds of reform; no such causal demonstration was established | Not supported |
| MSME support is a useful first review cluster | Historical mapping and an existing inventory mechanism offer a practical starting point | Supported for investigation; current closure case absent |
| AI time savings automatically reduce public expenditure | Costs fall only if spending is avoided or removed after all added costs | Rejected as an accounting assumption |
| Some agencies should eventually be disbanded | Plausible where the closure test is met | Conditional policy proposal; no named agency qualified by this study |

These are exploratory evidence assessments. They are not preregistered experiments, a systematic review, or independent performance audits.

Two final targeted checks examined (1) current institutions and the superseding readiness-index version and (2) merger costs and benefit accounting. They resolved wording and comparison risks but did not establish a defensible named closure or a Malaysian net-savings estimate. Conclude this desk-research phase; deepen with agency-level records before making those stronger claims.

## 10. The journal angle worth developing

**Working title:** Could AI Help Malaysia Build a Leaner Government?

**Central question:** If AI lets government share more work, which procedures, programmes and institutions still earn their cost?

**Provisional argument:** Malaysia should connect AI adoption to a review of how services are organised. Shared capabilities and measured outcomes can expose unnecessary duplication. Agency closure should follow evidence that useful services survive and net value improves.

**Original contribution:** extend KRI's coordination diagnosis into a concrete functional-review and savings framework, using MSME support as an investigable case.

**Evidence still needed for a stronger essay:** current programme-level spending and outcomes, agency statutes and audited accounts, contracts and workforce obligations, documented beneficiary journeys, evaluations of actual AI use, and responses from affected institutions and service users. These could support an agency-specific follow-up. They are not needed to explain the conditional framework honestly.
