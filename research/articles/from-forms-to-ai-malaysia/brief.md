# Malaysia's next economic transition: topic development

Date: 2026-10-22, Asia/Kuala_Lumpur
Status: exploratory topic shortlist; no article drafted or approved.

## Author-supplied starting point

Meor says he has worked in software since 2010 and witnessed movement from desktop and database-centred tools, including Oracle Forms, Microsoft Access, Microsoft Publisher and VBA, towards web development. Preserve this as his observed environment, not a universal product chronology. Publisher is a publishing application; do not describe every named tool as a database platform. He observed job scopes being removed, people moving to different roles, and new roles appearing during company and government digitisation projects. No specific project, employer, number of affected workers or outcome was supplied.

He sees a possible parallel between working with AI and the advantage of using a PC for work around 2000. This is an author analogy to examine, not empirical proof of identical transitions. He asks how Malaysia can advance with less policy friction and avoid another perceived MyTeksi/Grab loss to Singapore. Do not treat inevitable AGI, universal productivity gains, policy delay as the principal obstacle, or regulation as the proven cause of Grab's relocation as established facts.

## Creative-thinking application

Use problem reformulation, examination of the historical analogy, and movement between the national question and concrete tasks. These are prompts for investigation, not a validated economic model. No causal diagram, forecast or scenario matrix is proposed.

Reformulations:
- From 'what sector comes after manufacturing?' to 'how is work changing within Malaysia's existing sectors?'
- From 'how do we catch Singapore?' to 'which comparable outcomes and delivery processes should we examine?'
- From 'will workers use AI?' to 'where does measured quality-adjusted output improve, and what happens to the people doing the work?'

### T022 — Is AI Becoming the New PC at Work?
Reading/review angle. Reader question: Where does the desktop-computer analogy illuminate AI-assisted work, and where does measured evidence challenge it?
Review actual tasks, output quality, worker experience and deployment conditions. A productivity gain in one customer-support setting cannot predict every worker's advantage or Malaysia's employment balance. Do not make AGI necessary to the argument.
Distinct contribution: test the author's analogy rather than using it as a forecast. Evidence readiness: mixed results identified; deeper appraisal needed. Effort: medium.

### T023 — Beyond Another Grab: What Would Make Malaysian Firms Choose to Scale Here?
Investigative essay. Reader question: What do the records establish about MyTeksi's relocation, and which lessons can responsibly inform current policy questions?
Inspect founder accounts, investor records, contemporary funding announcements and specific rules. Distinguish headquarters, ownership, operating footprint, employment and where value is created. An investor's account is interested testimony, not independent proof of every motive.
Distinct contribution: test a familiar national story against primary records. Regulation-only explanation remains unsubstantiated. Evidence readiness: useful primary lead; incomplete causal history. Effort: large.

### T024 — From an AI Pilot to Everyday Work: A Malaysian Project Diary
Conditional field note, not an experiment already performed. Reader question: What actually changes when one documented Malaysian workflow adopts AI?
Only proceed with a real author-supplied case and permission to describe relevant details. Record actual dates, task completion, quality/rework and observed changes to roles if available; leave unavailable measurements blank. A single case cannot establish national effects.
Distinct contribution: concrete experience rather than a national forecast. Evidence readiness: no case supplied. Effort: depends on the case.

A research organisation reporting its own work; bounded population and tools.
