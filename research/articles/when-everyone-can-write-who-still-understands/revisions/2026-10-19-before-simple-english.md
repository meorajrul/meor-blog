I want AI to be the good guy in this story. A capable harness can help us work through information and produce useful material. [Noy and Zhang's writing experiment](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/productivity-effects-generative-artificial-intelligence) reported faster, better-rated output in bounded tasks. That is a benefit worth keeping.

My struggle is with what happens afterwards. Colleagues rely heavily on AI and produce far more work, yet struggle to make decisions from it. Some have not even read the output they pass along, but still expect an action plan. The document arrives; the responsibility for turning it into action remains unclear.

That is where I want us to walk the talk. Whoever owns the work should be able to explain the recommendation, question its assumptions and propose what happens next.

Google Maps and Waze give me an analogy: following directions can get us somewhere without ensuring we understand the route. A [2024 review of 23 navigation studies](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvp.2024.102417), with 18 pooled quantitatively, found small negative relationships with environmental knowledge and self-rated sense of direction; wayfinding was inconclusive. Most studies had moderate or high bias risk. These findings do not establish general reasoning decline, app-specific harm or an equivalent effect from AI.

![Published pooled results: environmental knowledge r=-0.18, self-reported sense of direction r=-0.25, and inconclusive wayfinding r=0.07. Lines show 95% confidence intervals.](gps-navigation-research.png)

*Original chart of published estimates from Miola et al. (2024), section 3.3. Correlations are not percentages or before-and-after declines. [Chart data](gps-navigation-effects.csv).*

The workplace question this raises for me is who understands enough to act when guidance is readily available. My proposed response is to connect AI-assisted work to clear direction, ownership and KPIs:

- **Set the direction.** Leadership defines the priority, intended outcome and what successful delivery means.
- **Assign an owner.** Give each defined job scope a named accountable person, clear decision authority and visible dependencies.
- **Require an owned plan.** That person reads and checks the output, brings a recommendation, and identifies actions, deadlines and unresolved decisions.
- **Measure follow-through.** Agree KPIs around accepted outcomes, milestones and completed commitments. A generated document alone would not count as delivery.

Ownership includes raising blockers outside your authority early enough for someone to help. Leaders also need to resolve conflicting priorities and provide the authority the scope requires. Otherwise, attaching a KPI would leave the underlying confusion intact. This is an approach I want to apply, not a result I can claim to have demonstrated.

The AI harness can support this discipline. [Skills package reusable instructions and resources](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills), including a procedure for checking sources and preparing a handoff. The agent still needs the current brief. [Context-engineering guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) describes retrieving material and compressing long interactions; excessive compression can lose important details. A skipped check needs investigation, not an automatic diagnosis of “laziness.”

Iqra, the call to read in [Qur'an 96:1](https://quran.com/96/1), gives this a personal meaning for me. [Hasrizal also connects Iqra and AI with verification and accountability](https://hasrizal.substack.com/p/from-iqra-to-the-age-of-ai-what-al). My practical commitment is to read, understand and take responsibility for the work within my scope. AI can help prepare the material; I still need to own the next action.
