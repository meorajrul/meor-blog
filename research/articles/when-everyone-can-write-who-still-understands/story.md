This is an organizing aid, not evidence for KPI effectiveness. The labels hero/conflict/resolution are editorial metaphors rather than factual judgments that colleagues are bad people or platforms infallible.

Supporting reasons: reusable AI procedures still need current task facts; producing a report leaves a separate question about who can act on it; work ownership needs authority and visible dependencies as well as accountability. These last organizational statements are proposed principles, not findings from the navigation paper.

Strongest alternative: a colleague may lack authority, clear priorities or sufficient context rather than willingness to act. A reasonable escalation can be evidence of ownership. The proposed KPI approach should use agreed outcomes within scope and recognize dependencies, without rewarding document volume or unapproved decisiveness.

What would change the argument: an actual workflow review might show that management direction or blocked authority is the limiting factor. Revise the ownership design accordingly. A future implementation would need real observations before any improvement claim.
